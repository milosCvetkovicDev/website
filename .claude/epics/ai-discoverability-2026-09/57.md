# Task: One connected structured-data graph, and the two rich results the site is eligible for

## Description

A machine reading this site finds two JSON-LD nodes with no relationship between them: a `Person` and a
`WebSite`, both emitted from the root layout, neither carrying an `@id`, neither referring to the other. No page
declares itself as a node at all, so the pages float free of the person they are about. Meanwhile two rich
results that Google still ships are sitting unused on the three case-study routes: `BreadcrumbList`, which
replaces the raw URL in the result with a navigational trail and needs only the hierarchy the pages already
have, and `Article` (here `TechArticle`), which gets date and title treatment once #56 supplies real dates.
This task wires every node into one `@id`-addressed graph, adds a `ProfilePage` on `/about` whose `mainEntity`
is that one `Person`, expands the `Person` with the facts `/about` actually displays, and emits `TechArticle`
and `BreadcrumbList` on the case studies. It is worth doing for exactly two reasons, and the ADR says so in
those words: two live Google rich results, and entity resolution. It is not an AI-citation lever — Google
states that structured data is not required for generative AI search and that there is no special schema.org
markup to add, and Ahrefs' matched difference-in-differences over 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD found no
citation lift. Every fact the markup asserts therefore also has to be visible on the page, which is both
Google's own structured-data policy and the only shape the measured evidence says extractors read.

Branch: feat/structured-data-graph. Lands as 2 PRs, squash-merged.

## Measures

### BreadcrumbList on the three case-study routes

- **What:** a `BreadcrumbJsonLd({ items })` block rendered from `apps/web/src/app/work/[slug]/page.tsx` with
  three `ListItem`s in order — Home, Work, the case-study title — each carrying `position`, `name` and an
  absolute `item` URL, and an `@id` of `<canonical>#breadcrumb` referenced from that route's `WebPage` node.
  Not emitted on `/work`, which has only one level and is not eligible.
- **Consumed by:** Google Search, which renders it as a navigational trail in place of the raw URL; Bing
  consumes schema.org generally. No answer engine documents reading it.
- **Evidence:** vendor-documented — Google's Breadcrumb reference, last updated 2026-09-08 (at least two
  `ListItem`s with `position`, `name`, `item`), and its structured-data gallery, last updated 2026-06-15, where
  Breadcrumb is one of the 31 supported features.
- **Done when:** all three case-study routes serve the block, Google's Rich Results Test detects Breadcrumbs
  with zero errors on each, and the verdicts are pasted into the pull request.

### TechArticle on the case studies, with dates that match the visible text

- **What:** a `TechArticleJsonLd({ caseStudy })` block per case study carrying `headline`, `description`,
  `datePublished`, `dateModified` (from #56's fields), `author` as an `@id` reference to the one `Person`,
  `mainEntityOfPage` pointing at the route's `WebPage` node and `isPartOf` pointing at the `WebSite`. Article
  has no required properties, so `image` is omitted unless the owner supplies a real file.
- **Consumed by:** Google Search — the Article rich result gives a result enhanced title, image and date
  treatment. Microsoft states for AI answers that regular updates help AI systems reference the current version
  of a page, which is a freshness claim about the visible dates, not about this markup.
- **Evidence:** vendor-documented — Google's Article reference, last updated 2026-09-08; the publication-dates
  guidance, last updated 2025-12-10 (a prominent user-visible labelled date, matching the structured value, no
  future dates); Microsoft's Bing Webmaster AI Performance announcement, 2026-02-10. That dates drive AI
  citation specifically is **not** claimed: no vendor documents it.
- **Done when:** each case study's `datePublished` and `dateModified` equal the dates #56 renders on the
  page, the Rich Results Test detects Article with zero errors on all three routes, and `TechArticle` — not
  `Article` — is the type, because these read as technical write-ups and Google's policies forbid mislabelling
  content type.

### One `@id`-connected graph: Person, WebSite and a WebPage node per route

- **What:** `Person` gets `@id: <origin>/#person`, `WebSite` gets `@id: <origin>/#website` plus `inLanguage`
  and `author`/`publisher` as `@id` references to the Person; a new `WebPageJsonLd` gives every route a
  `@id: <canonical>#webpage` node with `url`, `name`, `isPartOf` → the WebSite and `about` → the Person. No node
  repeats a fact another node owns; references are `{'@id': …}` objects.
- **Consumed by:** Google, for entity understanding — its ProfilePage and Article references both instruct
  authors to use `url`/`sameAs` on a Person "for disambiguation", which is an explicit statement that Google
  resolves person entities from markup. Answer engines: nothing reads it as structured data; searchVIU's
  direct-fetch test found JSON-LD extracted by none of five systems while visible HTML was extracted.
- **Evidence:** vendor-documented for the mechanism (ProfilePage and Article references, both last updated
  2026-09-08). Measured **null** as a citation lever: Ahrefs' matched difference-in-differences on 1,885 pages
  that added JSON-LD against ~4,000 controls, published 2026-05-11, found AI Overviews −4.6%, AI Mode +2.2%,
  ChatGPT +2.2% — the last two indistinguishable from noise. No knowledge panel is claimed: Google's gallery
  has no `Person` feature and no Google document describes markup as a route into the Knowledge Graph.
- **Done when:** every node on every route has an `@id`, every cross-reference resolves to an `@id` present on
  that page, and a unit test asserts both.

### ProfilePage on /about, with a visible labelled last-updated date

- **What:** a `ProfilePageJsonLd` rendered only from `apps/web/src/app/about/page.tsx`, with `mainEntity` as an
  `@id` reference to the one Person, `name` as the real name, `dateModified`, and `isPartOf` → the WebSite. It
  _is_ that route's WebPage node — `/about` does not emit both. `/about` also gains one visible, labelled
  "Last updated <date>" line, because the markup may not assert what the page does not show. Both wait on the
  owner's real date: until it lands the field holds #56's placeholder, registered in that task's gate, and
  neither the line nor the `dateModified` property renders (AC 9).
- **Consumed by:** Google, which documents `ProfilePage` for "a page that primarily focuses on information
  about a single person" and names About-me pages as a valid use. The feature its own documentation names is
  Discussions and Forums plus author linkage — **not** a standalone rich result for a one-person site, so none
  is promised here.
- **Evidence:** vendor-documented for the markup (ProfilePage reference, last updated 2026-09-08); speculative
  as an effect on this site, and recorded as an open question in the ADR. The widely-repeated claim that a
  `dateModified` older than 90 days forfeits a ProfilePage rich result appears in no Google document and must
  not drive a cadence.
- **Done when:** `/about` serves the ProfilePage node, the visible date string and `dateModified` agree, and the
  Rich Results Test detects Profile Page with zero errors.

### A Person expanded only to what /about displays

- **What:** add `alternateName` (the handle from the shared social module), `address` as a `PostalAddress` with
  the locality and country `/about` shows, `hasCredential` as an `EducationalOccupationalCredential` naming the
  certification `/about` shows, `hasOccupation` as an `Occupation` with `occupationLocation`, and
  `mainEntityOfPage` → the `/about` ProfilePage. `knowsAbout` is **trimmed** to entries that appear in the
  served text of at least one route. `worksFor` and `alumniOf` are not asserted: the timeline on `/about`
  anonymises employers, so there is nothing displayed to mark up.
- **Consumed by:** `sameAs` and `url` — Google, documented for disambiguation. `hasCredential`,
  `hasOccupation` and `knowsAbout` — **nothing reads them by default**: they are current schema.org vocabulary
  with no documented consumer in any search or answer engine, and Google's gallery has no `Person` feature at
  all.
- **Evidence:** convention-only, stated as such. The vocabulary is live (schema.org v30.0, released
  2026-03-19, which added a broader `Credential` class behind `hasCredential`; the W3C Educational and
  Occupational Credentials community group produced it and names no consuming implementation). The constraint
  is vendor-documented: Google's structured-data policies, last updated 2026-07-10, say "Don't mark up content
  that is not visible to readers of the page" and require markup to be a true representation of the page.
- **Done when:** every string the Person asserts is found by a test in the served HTML of at least one route —
  as extracted text for the facts, as a rendered link `href` for the profiles — and the pull request lists each
  asserted fact next to the route that displays it.

### Typed payloads, so a misspelled predicate fails the build

- **What:** the node payloads move out of JSX into the importable module #55 introduces for its offline
  structured-data gate, typed with `schema-dts` (`WithContext<Person>`, `WithContext<TechArticle>`,
  `WithContext<ProfilePage>`, `WithContext<BreadcrumbList>`, `WithContext<WebPage>`), and every block renders
  through #48's `serializeJsonLd`. No second escaping path is added.
- **Consumed by:** nothing external — this is a gate, and it is the reason the four new node types can be
  reviewed at all.
- **Evidence:** measured — a `jobTitel`-for-`jobTitle` typo returns `errorType: INVALID_PREDICATE` at ERROR
  severity from validator.schema.org, probed 2026-09-12; the offline half is a plain `pnpm typecheck` with no
  network. `schema-dts` v2.0.0 was published 2026-03-23. The validator itself stays advisory: it is an
  undocumented endpoint whose response carries a `)]}'` XSSI prefix, and it validates schema.org vocabulary,
  not Google rich-result eligibility, which only the Rich Results Test answers and which has no API.
- **Done when:** `pnpm typecheck` fails on a deliberately misspelled predicate (output pasted into the pull
  request) and passes on the real payloads.

**The graph shape.** Two script blocks stay in the layout (Person, WebSite) and each route adds its own, with
cross-references by `@id`. Separate blocks are deliberate: collapsing everything into a single `@graph` would
hide the top-level `@type` that #43's e2e assertion reads and would reduce the block count that same spec
pins, for no gain — the research's own conclusion is that `@id` references across the existing blocks are what
turns two unrelated nodes into one connected graph. Skeleton, with `serializeJsonLd` doing the escaping:

```ts
// layout: emitted on every route
{ '@context': 'https://schema.org', '@type': 'Person',
  '@id': `${siteUrl}/#person`,
  name: 'Milos Cvetkovic', alternateName: <handle from data/social.ts>, url: siteUrl,
  jobTitle: …, description: <from data/profile.ts, so the years figure has one home>,
  address: { '@type': 'PostalAddress', addressLocality: …, addressCountry: … },
  hasCredential: { '@type': 'EducationalOccupationalCredential', name: …, credentialCategory: 'certification' },
  hasOccupation: { '@type': 'Occupation', name: …, occupationLocation: { '@type': 'City', name: … } },
  knowsAbout: [ …only entries visible somewhere on the site… ],
  sameAs: <data/social.ts hrefs>,
  mainEntityOfPage: { '@id': `${siteUrl}/about#webpage` } }

{ '@context': 'https://schema.org', '@type': 'WebSite',
  '@id': `${siteUrl}/#website`, name: …, url: siteUrl, description: …, inLanguage: 'en',
  author: { '@id': `${siteUrl}/#person` }, publisher: { '@id': `${siteUrl}/#person` } }

// every route except /about
{ '@context': 'https://schema.org', '@type': 'WebPage',
  '@id': `${canonical}#webpage`, url: canonical, name: <the route's title>,
  isPartOf: { '@id': `${siteUrl}/#website` }, about: { '@id': `${siteUrl}/#person` } }

// /about only — ProfilePage IS its WebPage node
{ '@context': 'https://schema.org', '@type': 'ProfilePage',
  '@id': `${siteUrl}/about#webpage`, url: …, name: 'Milos Cvetkovic',
  dateModified: <owner value; the property is omitted entirely while #56's placeholder survives>,
  isPartOf: { '@id': `${siteUrl}/#website` }, mainEntity: { '@id': `${siteUrl}/#person` } }

// /work/[slug] only, alongside its WebPage (which gains breadcrumb: { '@id': `${canonical}#breadcrumb` })
{ '@context': 'https://schema.org', '@type': 'TechArticle',
  '@id': `${canonical}#article`, headline: caseStudy.title, description: caseStudy.description,
  datePublished: caseStudy.datePublished, dateModified: caseStudy.dateModified,
  author: { '@id': `${siteUrl}/#person` }, isPartOf: { '@id': `${siteUrl}/#website` },
  mainEntityOfPage: { '@id': `${canonical}#webpage` } }

{ '@context': 'https://schema.org', '@type': 'BreadcrumbList',
  '@id': `${canonical}#breadcrumb`,
  itemListElement: [
    { '@type': 'ListItem', position: 1, name: 'Home', item: `${siteUrl}/` },
    { '@type': 'ListItem', position: 2, name: 'Work', item: `${siteUrl}/work` },
    { '@type': 'ListItem', position: 3, name: caseStudy.title, item: canonical } ] }
```

`canonical` is #48's per-route canonical value, read from the same place its `alternates.canonical` reads, never
rebuilt from a literal. `/work` keeps a plain `WebPage` rather than a `CollectionPage`: no document in the
research distinguishes a consumer for the subtype, and the simpler shape is easier to keep true. `image` is
omitted from every node unless the owner supplies a real file under `apps/web/public`: do not point it at
#48's generated Open Graph route, whose URL is cache-busted with a content hash (measured on a probe of this
repository, 2026-09-12, as `…/work/self-healing-agent/opengraph-image?<hash>`), so a hand-written path would
rot silently. Article has no required properties, so omitting it costs nothing.

**Next.js 16 specifics, and the traps that bind this area.** This task adds no route handler and must not add
one — the nodes belong in the prerendered HTML head, and a `/schema.json` endpoint would be a second home for
facts the head already carries. The traps matter anyway, because the next three tasks land in the same files:
route handlers are **not** static by default (the default caching for `GET` changed from static to dynamic in
15.0.0-RC, and a probe of this repository on 2026-09-12 built `○ /llms.txt` with
`export const dynamic = 'force-static'` and `ƒ /llms-full.txt` without it — one missing line is a function per
crawl); `force-static` is removed **only** when `cacheComponents` is enabled (Next 16.0.0), which this
repository does not set, so it is the correct mechanism today and reading its absence from the main
route-segment-config table as removal is the mistake; and `[slug].md` cannot work, because a partial dynamic
segment types as a literal with zero params (`RouteHandlerConfig<"/work/[slug].md">` with `params: Promise<{}>`),
which is why #59's twins are `/work/<slug>/index.md`. The build-output classification (`○` static, `●`
SSG, `ƒ` dynamic) is the verifiable stop condition, but the table's formatting is not a stable API — Next 16
redesigned terminal output — so assert on emitted `.body`/`.meta` artifacts, which is what #55's gate
does. One more Next behaviour to respect: metadata merges shallowly, a page-level `alternates` replaces an
ancestor's, so this task reads #48's canonical rather than setting `alternates` anywhere.

- **`SearchAction` / sitelinks search box** — deprecation announced 2024-10-21, retired globally 2024-11-21;
  the markup is inert, and the site has no search anyway. Half the circulating "personal site schema"
  templates still ship it.
- **An `Organization` node, `worksFor`, `alumniOf`** — the site is one person and `/about` anonymises its
  timeline, so these would assert what the page does not display, against Google's structured-data policies
  (last updated 2026-07-10).
- **JSON Resume, `cv.json`, `/.well-known/agent-card.json`** — no documented consumer among search engines,
  answer engines or ATS vendors; the most careful public test explicitly ran no live ATS submission or
  recruiter-agent test. Also a privacy decision. Out of scope per the PRD.
- **A self-created Wikidata item** — notability would very likely reject an item for a solo engineer, and a
  deleted self-created item is worse than none. Out of scope per the PRD.
- **Any claim that this markup earns AI citations, a knowledge panel, or a ProfilePage rich result** — Google
  states structured data is not required for generative AI search and that there is no special schema.org
  markup to add (AI optimization guide, last updated 2026-07-10); the gallery has no `Person` feature
  (2026-06-15); the ProfilePage reference names Discussions and Forums and author linkage, not a standalone
  rich result. The 90-day `dateModified` rule circulating in SEO posts exists in no Google document.

**Owner decisions that apply.** Dates land as placeholders that fail CI and are filled during review — nothing
fabricated ships, so the `/about` last-updated value is #56's placeholder from the first commit, registered
in that task's `unfilledOwnerFields` with a `why` and the owner's `expires` rather than guarded by a new check.

Sequencing: this task starts only after #48 has landed, and builds on its canonical URLs and `serializeJsonLd`.

agent may invent — and then the real `/about` last-updated date, which clears the row. Confirmation of the exact
credential name and whether it stays displayed on `/about`, since the node may assert only what the page shows.

profile the owner actually controls, added to #49's social module rather than to the `sameAs` array directly.

  the years figure, the dates and the canonicals all come from their owning module); consume #48's helpers and
